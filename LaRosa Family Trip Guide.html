First RV Trip: What to Know

Trip at a glance

Six nights, Sun Nov 22 to Sat Nov 28, with Scruffy, in a 24 ft 2020 Thor Freedom Elite 22HE (gas, pet friendly, 4 seatbelts; linens, kitchen kit and camp chairs included), picked up and returned in Damascus: about 1,345 RV miles. Gold Bluffs and Fern Canyon are out, because Davison Road only allows rigs up to 24 ft.

Good to know

  • Firewood: don't bring it from Oregon. Buy it where you burn it; expect a California agriculture inspection stop that asks about wood and produce.

  • Fires: ask each campground before using the Takibi, and check for burn restrictions that week.

  • Saturday pickup: unload everything at home Friday night, before the owner collects the RV.

  • Dogs in the redwoods: dogs aren't allowed on trails in Redwood National and State Parks, Founders Grove or Richardson Grove. They're fine in campgrounds, on roads and leashed on many beaches, including Harris Beach and Glass Beach. On redwood hikes, take turns staying with Scruffy or leave him in the cool RV.

Car fun (no screens)

About 31 hours of driving, most of it on Sunday night, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday. Keep a "fun bag" within reach and rotate things in and out so nothing gets old.

Trip-specific games

  • Redwood Coast bingo: make cards before you go. Squares: elk, Paul Bunyan, a drive-thru tree, a lighthouse, sea lions, a banana slug, Bigfoot (statue or sign), the "Welcome to California" sign, the agriculture inspection station, an RV like ours, a covered bridge, a fishing boat, fog, a rainbow, a tree wider than our RV.

  • Elk and Bigfoot points: 1 point per elk, 5 for a bull with antlers, 10 for any Bigfoot. Running total all week; winner picks Friday's lunch spot.

  • Navigator of the day: each kid takes a turn with a paper map and a highlighter, tracing the route and announcing the next town and how many miles to go.

  • Guess the miles: everyone guesses how far to the next stop; closest gets first pick of snacks.

  • Junior Rangers: pick up free Redwood National Park Junior Ranger booklets at a visitor center Monday or Tuesday and work on them in the car; get sworn in at the next visitor center.

Talking and story games

  • Would you rather, redwoods edition: live in a hollow redwood or a lighthouse? Be an elk or a banana slug?

  • Fortunately / unfortunately: take turns adding a line to a story ("Fortunately we found a giant tree. Unfortunately it was Bigfoot's house...").

  • 20 questions, I spy, the alphabet game (find A to Z on signs, in order) and Categories (name an animal for every letter).

  • Gratitude round on the drive into camp on Wednesday, as a warm-up for Thanksgiving.

Audio (great for Sunday night and Friday)

  • Audiobooks: The Wild Robot (a robot surviving in the wild; perfect for this trip), Pax, Island of the Blue Dolphins (set on the California coast), Charlotte's Web read by E.B. White, or the first Harry Potter.

Foraging

Crab, clams, urchins and oysters along the route. Rules differ between Oregon and California, and late-November openings depend on toxin testing, so check everything the week of the trip.

Licenses

  • Oregon: shellfish license for anyone 12 and older. Buy online at myodfw.com.

  • California: sport fishing license for anyone 16 and older; short-term 1- and 2-day licenses are available. Buy at wildlife.ca.gov/Licensing/Fishing. Crab traps need a separate validation; hoop nets and crab snares don't.

  • Kids under those ages don't need a license, but their catch counts toward limits.

Safety checks

  • Marine toxins: call the Oregon Department of Agriculture shellfish safety hotline (800-448-2474) and the California Department of Public Health shellfish line (800-553-4133) the week you go. Mussels and razor clams close often in fall.

  • California crab season: check CDFW's Dungeness crab news for openings and gear limits.

  • Marine protected areas: taking anything is banned in many California reserves, including spots near Fort Bragg. Check the CDFW MPA map before collecting.

  • Crab: eat the meat only; throw out the guts ("crab butter").

  • Limits and sizes: carry a crab gauge and keep only legal-size males.

Sources

  • ODFW: razor clam closure

  • ODA: south coast mussel closures

Highlights up close

Trees of Mystery (Klamath, Mon). A roadside classic since the 1940s: a 49-foot Paul Bunyan and 35-foot Babe the Blue Ox greet you at the entrance. A paved trail winds through old-growth redwoods, and the SkyTrail gondola rides 1,570 feet up through the canopy, at times more than 500 feet above the forest floor, to an ocean-and-forest viewpoint. The End of the Trail Museum in the gift shop is free. Admission is listed at about $30 for adults and $15 for kids 3–12; confirm before you go. Plan 1–2 hours.

Blue Ox Millworks (Eureka, Tue). A working woodshop where craftsmen make Victorian-style trim and millwork with antique machinery, for clients from historic cathedrals to national parks. Tour the shop, a logging skid camp, traditional boat-building, and meet the blue oxen, Babe and Blue. Founder Eric Hollenbeck stars in the series The Craftsman. Call ahead for hours, and ask for a crew-led tour.

Hadley Gardens (Ferndale, Tue). A small, whimsical garden at 655 Main Street with a Victorian replica dollhouse, living walls, a waterfall, and hobbit doors for the kids to find. A quick, free-feeling stop on Ferndale's Victorian main street, close to the Cat Shack for ice cream.

Founders Grove (Humboldt Redwoods, Tue). A flat half-mile loop off the Avenue of the Giants through some of the tallest trees on Earth. The highlight is the Dyerville Giant, a redwood more than 360 feet tall that fell in 1991. Walking its full length gives a sense of how big these trees really are, and the root ball is taller than the van.

Glass Beach (Fort Bragg, Wed). Decades of old town dumps along the shore were worn by the waves into smooth, colorful sea glass. Glass Beach #1 is the easiest walk from the parking lot; #2, south along the bluff trail, often has more glass. It's part of MacKerricher State Park, so taking glass is illegal: make it a spotting game for the rarest color. Go at low tide, wear sturdy shoes, and watch for sneaker waves.

Laguna Point boardwalk (MacKerricher SP, Wed). A short, flat boardwalk through coastal dunes and wetlands to a rocky point where harbor seals haul out on the rocks below. It's easy for everyone, and a good spot to scan for early gray whales heading south. Just north of Fort Bragg, a few minutes from Glass Beach.

Sources: Trees of Mystery · Trees of Mystery prices · Blue Ox Millworks · Hadley Gardens · Glass Beach

Van know-how

The van is shorter than most moving trucks, so the new part is living in it: water, power, heat and tanks.

Before you leave

  • Ask the host for a Saturday-evening pickup, then pack it and sleep in it once in the driveway

  • Film the host walkthrough: filling water, dumping gray and black tanks, heater, water heater, fridge, battery and propane gauges, generator, toilet, breaker and fuse panel

  • Confirm the fuel type (many camper vans are diesel Sprinters) and real mpg

  • Confirm the mileage allowance and overage rate

  • Ask whether Davison Road (gravel, stream crossings) is allowed

  • Ask about chains or traction tires; Hwy 199 over Hayes Hill and the I-5 passes north of Grants Pass can ice up in late November

  • Ask about generator rules and whether bedding, kitchen gear and leveling blocks are included

  • Ask what the return in Vancouver requires (fuel level, empty tanks, cleaning) so Saturday's buffer covers it

  • Measure the van's height (usually 9–10 ft) and stick it on the dash

  • Download offline maps and print tide tables; tell someone the route

Driving the van

Drive it like a loaded moving truck, with extra care on slow roads and in coastal wind.

  • Downhills: on Hwy 199 and the I-5 passes, shift to a lower gear instead of riding the brakes.

  • Wind: a tall van gets pushed around on exposed coast and bridges; slow down.

  • Davison Road (Gold Bluffs): 8 miles of single-lane gravel with two stream crossings; walking pace, watch for elk. Limit is 24 ft long, 8 ft wide.

  • Hwy 1 north of Fort Bragg: steep and curvy between Leggett and the coast; take it slow.

  • Backing in: one person outside as spotter, with hand signals agreed beforehand. Most sites are back-in.

  • Kids: ride only in belted seats; nobody walks around while moving.

  • Daylight: sunset is about 4:50pm. Check TripCheck and Caltrans QuickMap for ice before the passes.

  • Fuel: fill up in Fort Bragg and Eureka; fuel is cheaper in Oregon, so top off before the border.

Living in the van

Water and battery are the two things that run out; check both every morning.

  • Water: small vans hold about 20–30 gallons. Use campground bathrooms and showers, and fill up at every spigot. Bring two jugs for Gold Bluffs.

  • Tanks: dump at Honey Bear, Westport (sewer at the site), Hendy Woods and Seven Feathers. Arrive at Gold Bluffs empty.

  • Heat and power: nights will be about 35–50°F. The furnace usually burns propane but its fan runs on the battery. Gold Bluffs and Hendy Woods have no hookups; Honey Bear, two nights at Westport and Seven Feathers recharge the battery in between.

  • Condensation: four people in a small van fog the windows overnight. Crack a window or run the roof fan low.

  • Quiet hours: state parks run roughly 10pm–6am, with set generator hours.

  • Cell service: none at Westport (Starlink Wi-Fi there) and spotty in the redwoods. Offline maps, and a plan for meeting up if separated.

  • Holiday week: small-town shops close Thanksgiving Day; buy food Wednesday.

Fires and cooking

Cook on the gas stove and use the Takibi mainly as a charcoal grill; wood fires usually belong in the campground's own fire ring.

  • Takibi: California state parks usually require wood fires in the provided ring. Ask each campground before burning wood in the Takibi, and check for burn restrictions that week.

  • Firewood: don't bring it from Oregon. Buy it where you burn it; expect a California agriculture inspection stop that asks about wood and produce.

  • Stove fuel: pack every canister you need; small coastal stores may not carry your size.

  • Propane: turn the van's propane off when fueling.

  • Cook outside the van when you can, never leave a flame unattended, and keep the Takibi well clear of the van and awning.
